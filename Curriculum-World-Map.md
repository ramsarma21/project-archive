# Curriculum World Map — covering all 85 STAAR 8 SEs across a handful of settings

**Status: design authority for the multi-chapter curriculum plan.** Companion to `Boston-STAAR-Coverage-Map.md` (which details Boston's slice). This doc answers: *how do we cover the **entire** STAAR 8 U.S. History curriculum (all 85 assessed SEs, colonization → 1877) without stretching any one world past what it can authentically carry?*

## Spiral vs. event-anchored SEs (how chapters share the load)

Not every SE is "taught once and done." Two kinds:

- **ONCE (event-anchored):** named, era-specific content owned outright by one chapter (e.g., the Boston Tea Party (20)(B), Lexington & Concord (4)(C), the Constitutional Convention (5)). Assessed only at that chapter's checkpoints.
- **SPIRAL (thematic):** the Geography/Economics/Government/Citizenship/Culture strands + "eras" (1)(A) — STAAR assesses these with content from *any* era. A single chapter can only **introduce** them; pattern-level mastery forms through **deliberate re-encounter across chapters.**

**Design rules this creates:**
1. **Each chapter re-teaches the spiral standards in its own era** — never "Boston already did representative government." Boston town meeting → Jamestown's House of Burgesses → Philadelphia's Convention are the *same* spiral SE (3)(A) seen three ways.
2. **Later checkpoints ask cross-era/compare questions** on spiral SEs (e.g., "compare representative government in Jamestown, Boston, and Philadelphia"), not just single-era recall.
3. **The STAAR bank tags each item ONCE or SPIRAL** so spiral coverage accumulates across chapters instead of duplicating.

Boston's own split: **5 ONCE** ((4)(A), (4)(B), (4)(C) Lexington/Concord, (20)(B), (23)(B)) + **18 SPIRAL**. Full per-SE recurrence tags live in `Boston-STAAR-Coverage-Map.md` → *"The exact target set."*

## Guiding principle — no square pegs

1. **Each setting is the *native home* of one era.** We never force an era's content into a place where it didn't happen. Boston carries the Revolution because the Revolution happened in Boston; it does **not** carry the antebellum South or the frontier.
2. **Use the Act / world-turn mechanic to span decades in one place.** The game already re-dresses a single town across years (Boston Acts 1–4, 1765→1775). Every chapter city inherits this: one built location ages across its era, so a *handful of locations* honestly spans 1607–1877.
3. **Reuse the asset kit wherever the period allows.** Two of the four new chapters are colonial-era and reuse Boston's brick/clapboard kit almost wholesale — huge production ROI.
4. **Completeness over breadth-per-chapter.** The goal is that *every* one of the 85 SEs lands in exactly one chapter where it's the authentic story — not that each chapter covers as much as possible.

## Chapter detail

### 0 — Boston *(built)* — the Revolution
- **Timeline:** A1 1765 Stamp Act · A2 1770 Massacre · A3 1773 Tea Party · A4 1774–75 Coercive Acts → Lexington & Concord → Siege of Boston.
- **Owns (Tier A, 11):** (4)(A) causes of the Revolution · (1)(A) eras · (3)(A) representative gov't (town meeting) · (10)(C) waterways/communication · (11)(A) geography→economy · (15)(C) grievances→Bill of Rights · (4)(B) individuals · (20)(B) Tea Party civil disobedience · (21)(A) points of view · (21)(B) free press · (23)(E) women.
- **Touches (Tier B, 12):** see `Boston-STAAR-Coverage-Map.md` target set. *(1776/Declaration moved to Philadelphia.)*
- **Expansion:** the **gated Lexington/Concord country-road corridor** — see §"Boston expansion" below and the geometry spec in `World-Design-Bible.md`.

### 1 — Philadelphia — the founding *(highest ROI new chapter)*
- **Setting:** Independence Hall / State House, the surrounding Georgian city, Penn's Quaker street grid, the 1790s national capital.
- **Timeline:** Act A 1776 (Second Continental Congress, Declaration) · Act B 1787 (Constitutional Convention) · Act C 1788–90s (ratification debate, Bill of Rights, Washington's presidency, first parties).
- **Owns (~15 Philadelphia-anchored):** **(4)(D)** Constitutional Convention (Great Compromise, Three-Fifths) · **(15)(B)** Articles of Confederation · **(15)(D)** Constitution principles (limited gov't, checks & balances, federalism, separation of powers, popular sovereignty) · **(16)(A)** amendment process · **(17)(A)** Federalists vs. Anti-Federalists · **(18)(A/B)** judicial review / Marbury / McCulloch · **(19)(B)** rights in the Bill of Rights · **(5)(A/C/D/F)** early-republic domestic problems, bank/tariffs, political parties, Washington's Farewell/foreign policy · **(25)(A/C)** development of religious freedom (Penn's Quaker colony) & First-Amendment freedom · **(1)(B)** dates 1776 + 1787.
- **Shares (~13 spiral, split cleanly from Boston by era):** (1)(A) eras · (3)(A) rep. gov't · (4)(B) individuals *(Philly cast: Jefferson, Franklin, Paine, Madison, Hamilton)* · (4)(C) *declaring independence half* · (15)(A) documents *(Philly adds the Federalist Papers)* · (15)(C) *how grievances were addressed in the Constitution/Bill of Rights* · (15)(E) Locke/Montesquieu · (19)(A) natural rights · (19)(C) citizenship · (20)(A) civic virtue · (21)(A) points of view · (21)(B) press · (22)(A) *Washington's presidency* · (23)(E) women · (12)/(14) new-nation economics.
- **Must NOT carry (Boston-only):** (4)(A) causes of the Revolution, (4)(C) Lexington & Concord, (20)(B) Boston Tea Party, (23)(B) urbanization→Massacre.
- **Total footprint ≈ 28 SEs** (~9 of the anchored ones are Readiness — the heaviest RC3 government cluster on the exam).
- **Carriers:** Franklin, Madison, Hamilton, Washington; the Federalist/Anti-Federalist ratification debate as a two-sided POV set-piece.
- **Asset ROI:** reuses Boston's colonial brick/clapboard kit almost entirely — a large civic hall (Independence Hall) is the main new hero build. **Cheapest chapter, densest government payoff.**

### 2 — Virginia Tidewater — colonization + the South *(double-duty)*
- **Setting:** one Tidewater place across three time layers — Jamestown fort / Williamsburg capital, and a James River plantation with quarters, fields, and a river landing.
- **Timeline:** Act A 1607–20s (Jamestown, tobacco, indentured servitude) · Act B ~1770 (Williamsburg, House of Burgesses = first elected assembly) · Act C 1850s (plantation South, King Cotton).
- **Owns colonization:** **(2)** European colonization, Jamestown, cash-crop economy, indentured servitude; **(3)** House of Burgesses as origin of representative gov't.
- **Owns the South:** plantation economy, **slavery & enslaved people's culture and resistance (23)**, **cotton gin / Eli Whitney (science-tech cluster)**, **King Cotton & regional economic differences (12)(13)(14)** — the pillar Boston (a northern merchant town) cannot authentically carry, and the human/economic root of the Civil War.
- **Asset ROI:** colonial-kit reuse for Williamsburg + **new** agrarian/plantation assets (great house, quarters, fields, tobacco/cotton props, river landing).

### 3 — Frontier & Trails — westward expansion
- **Setting:** a Mississippi/Missouri "gateway" town + an overland trail segment (prairie, river crossing, mountains on the skyline).
- **Timeline:** Act A 1803 (Louisiana Purchase, Lewis & Clark departure) · Act B 1830s (Jacksonian democracy, Indian Removal / Trail of Tears) · Act C 1846–49 (Manifest Destiny, Oregon/California trails, Mexican-American War, gold rush).
- **Owns:** **(7)** westward expansion & Manifest Destiny; **(6)** Jacksonian democracy & Indian Removal; migration & settlement **geography (9)(10)(11)**; frontier **free enterprise (14)**; transport **science/tech** (steamboat, railroad, telegraph).
- **Carriers:** Jackson, a displaced Cherokee family (Trail of Tears POV), trail guides, a gold-rush merchant.
- **Asset ROI:** **new kit** (log cabins, sod/prairie, covered wagons, frontier mercantile, river ferry).

### 4 — Civil War & Reconstruction — the union tested
- **Setting:** a border/Upper-South town (Gettysburg or Richmond feel) + an adjacent battlefield with earthworks and a bridge.
- **Timeline:** Act A 1861 (secession, Fort Sumter, mustering) · Act B 1863 (a major battle, Emancipation Proclamation) · Act C 1865–77 (surrender, 13/14/15th Amendments, Reconstruction & Freedmen's Bureau).
- **Owns:** **(8)** sectionalism causes (states' rights, slavery, tariffs), secession, key battles & leaders (Lincoln, Davis, Lee, Grant, Douglass), Emancipation, **13th/14th/15th Amendments**, Reconstruction; **(23)** Freedmen's experience; wartime **science/tech** (ironclads, rail logistics, telegraph).
- **Asset ROI:** **new kit** (war-era town, earthworks, artillery, field hospital, bridge).

**Recommended build order (coverage-per-dollar):**

2. **Philadelphia** — cheapest new chapter, unlocks the entire government-structure cluster (biggest RC3 gap).
3. **Virginia Tidewater** — unlocks colonization + the South + the economic/slavery root of the Civil War.
4. **Frontier & Trails** and **Civil War & Reconstruction** — the two new-kit chapters that close the back half of the timeline.

## Boston expansion — fitting as much as Boston honestly can

Boston's authentic ceiling is the **Revolution + founding-adjacent** content (the 23 SEs in the coverage map). We do **not** stretch it toward colonization-South, the Constitution, expansion, or the Civil War — those go to the chapters above. The only geographic expansion Boston needs is the **one true out-of-town gap: Lexington & Concord** (and, framed from the same edge, Washington's arrival at the **Siege of Boston**).

**Decision (locked): a small gated country-road corridor east of the town gate**, blocked by a diegetic barrier until Act 4 (1775), terminating in a compact **Lexington Green + Concord North Bridge** pocket. This is a narrow linear "chute," not a town expansion — it obeys the existing **route-gating law** (a route is state, not geometry) and the **harbor-exclusion rule** (it extends *east*, away from the west/southwest open water).

- **Build-ready geometry, bounds extension, blocker, and asset list:** see `World-Design-Bible.md` → *"Eastward expansion — the Lexington/Concord country road (A4 gated corridor)."*
- **Curriculum payoff:** unlocks (4)(C) Lexington & Concord + declaring-independence setup, and stages (22)(A) Washington / Siege — completing Boston's Tier-B revolution finale without a second explorable town.
