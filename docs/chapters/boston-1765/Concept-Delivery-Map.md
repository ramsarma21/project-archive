# Boston — Concept Delivery Map (gated facts vs. load-bearing patterns)

**Status: the scope + pacing bridge between the curriculum (`STAAR-Coverage-Map.md`) and the built learning machinery (`@pa/contracts`, `Day-1.md` §2C).** It tags each of Boston's 23 SEs with *how* it's delivered and *when* (which Act), so per-Act density stays near today's proven budget (~3 gated concepts / ~25–30 min).

## The delivery model (three classes)

Grounded in the design law: **facts are assessed; patterns are made load-bearing; the Archive is used wisely.**

| Class | What it is | How it's delivered | Assessed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **① GATED FACT** | Boston-specific facts, dates, people, events (the ONCE set) | The full built lifecycle: 3 typed tracked exposures → Archive **Sync** (Understanding) → in-world **demonstration** → MASTERED | **Yes** — Sync + demonstration; on the mastery report |
| **② LOAD-BEARING PATTERN** | Historical trends/patterns (free markets, oppression, representation, women's contributions…) | Baked into a **core gameplay system** so *you cannot play well without internalizing it.* The mechanic is the lesson; difficulty + consequences enforce it. | **Mostly no quiz** — proven by play. **Exception:** the few *highest-STAAR-value* patterns (the most-tested Readiness ones) also get a light **Archive Sync safety net.** |
| **③ AMBIENT / ARCHIVE-FRAMED** | Framing (eras, regions) + pure flavor | Archive era-framing at Act open (cheap) + ambient world chatter/dressing (zero cost, never tracked) | No |

**Design law for patterns (new):** *make the historical pattern the "physics" of a gameplay system.* If a player can succeed while ignoring the pattern, it's mis-delivered.

**Dual-delivered patterns (the lean exception):** patterns that STAAR tests most heavily and cross-era (the top Readiness ones) are taught by gameplay **and** get one light Archive Sync as a safety net. Kept deliberately small — **most patterns stay gameplay-only** for time/efficiency. Current dual set (~4): **(3)(A) representation · (19)(A) natural rights · (15)(C) grievances→rights · (11)(A) geography→economy.**

**Archive annoyance budget:** the Archive narrates Act prologues, hosts Syncs for gated facts + the ~4 dual patterns, and does occasional cross-era synthesis — but it is **not** used to quiz the majority of patterns. Cap Sync frequency (existing rule: ≥2 interactions between Syncs); prefer the world to teach.

## Delivery hierarchy — player involvement first

Ranked by how much the player *does*. Always deliver at the highest rung the content allows:

1. **Load-bearing mechanic (default).** The player is the actor; the history is the rules. Covers the vast majority of concepts.
2. **Interactive set-piece with a gameplay on-ramp.** For big beats — the player still climbs/pushes/chants/observes their way *into* the moment (route-invariant on-ramp law, `Day-1.md`).
3. **Sparing cutscene — STAAR-named marquee events ONLY.** Reserved for the Boston Massacre, the Tea Party, Lexington & Concord. Each still keeps an interactive on-ramp; pre-rendered cutscenes are never used for ordinary teaching.
4. **Archive subtle reinforcement (the implicit→explicit bridge).** Mechanics teach the *feeling*; STAAR tests the *label*. When a mechanic won't surface the vocabulary on its own, the Archive quietly names what the player just lived (*"those searches were 'writs of assistance' — general warrants; that's the grievance"*) — one line, tied to a concrete action, inside the annoyance budget. **Never a lecture, never a substitute for the mechanic.**

### Archive decision-framing (bracketing meaningful choices)

To keep choices *historically reasoned* (not "pick risky for fun"), the Archive brackets a **meaningful** decision (one with real state consequence) with two subtle, optional, skippable touches:

- **Before — the frame (a consideration, never the answer):** one clause that surfaces the historical cause→effect the choice hinges on:
  - *"(Clarke's a Loyalist — he reports what he sees.)"* → points of view (21)(A) + heat/Standing stakes
  - *"(A boycott only bites if the town holds together.)"* → non-importation / collective action
  - *"(The watch remembers faces.)"* → enforcement / persistent heat
- **After — the bridge (only if needed):** the implicit→explicit label for what happened (*"that's why the Sons of Liberty worked in secret"*).

**Rules:** fires only on choices that move state; one clause, opt-in tone, never "don't" and never the correct answer; obeys the annoyance budget (not every choice, dial-able). Effect: **risk becomes a historically-grounded gamble with real consequences** — the frame is simultaneously a learning delivery (the cause→effect logic) and the thing that makes decisions meaningful instead of chaotic.

**Vehicle-fit rule (dialogue rehabilitated):** required content uses the vehicle that fits it, not always a mechanic — **mechanic** (default when a natural verb exists), **paper/document** (proclamations, deeds, broadsides), **authored dialogue** (genuinely interpersonal content — a warning, testimony, a negotiation — that a mechanic would make contrived), **event/set-piece**, or a **sparing cutscene** (marquee only). The rule is *no dialogue **spam** as a lazy default* — not "no dialogue." **Ambient/overheard chatter** is the only thing that's pure seasoning (never a required vehicle).

## Required vs. optional — and the saturation law

Two independent axes govern every piece of content:

1. **Required vs. optional.**
   - **Required core** = must land on every path (gated facts + the load-bearing patterns that carry them). Guaranteed by the built **carrier + type-aware reroute** system and the **fully-avoidant-path** test: a player who only reads, only talks, only does, or skips optional content *still* reaches coverage.
   - **Optional depth** = discovery (environmental lore, threads, challenges, flavor). Never forced; curiosity-rewarded.

2. **The saturation law — *no activity is learning-empty.*** Every substantive activity carries a payload at one of three densities, and the **ambient layer is always on**, so there is no way to spend time without learning *something*:

| Activity | Req/Opt | Best-fit vehicle | Payload |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spine job / errand | **Required** | mechanic + paper + dialogue | macro (gated fact) |
| Marquee event (Massacre, Tea Party, Lexington) | **Required** | event / cutscene + on-ramp | macro (gated fact) |
| Key conversation (warning, testimony) | **Required** | authored dialogue | macro (fact / POV) |
| Contraband run · boycott · press · town-meeting | mixed | mechanic | pattern |
| Inspect found-history object | Optional | paper / focus-inspect | micro |
| NPC thread | Optional | dialogue + mechanic | pattern + arc |
| Challenge (stealth / traversal) | Optional | mechanic | pattern (world tension) |
| Flavor verb (ring bell, pet dog) | Optional | verb | fun — *rides on* the always-on ambient teaching |
| Ambient (overheard drama, visible town state) | **Always on** | passive | context — never zero |

**Design test for saturation:** if you can point to any 30 seconds of play that teaches *nothing* (not even ambient context), that surface is mis-designed. Fun-only flavor verbs are allowed *because* they happen inside a world whose ambient layer is always teaching.

## The creed

> **The pitch: GTA × Elden Ring in 1765 Boston, where everything teaches.** An open town you roam freely — interact, take quests, find things, run mechanics — with the whole learning engine built *into* the world, not bolted on. Freedom and consequence like GTA; discovery, density, and "always something to do" like Elden Ring; every surface a lesson.

> **Everything you see teaches you. Everything you do teaches you. Everything is fun. Everything you do matters.**

Four claims, and every feature is measured against all four. "See" = the ambient/environmental layer (saturation). "Do" = mechanics, quests, routes (learning baked into rules). "Fun" = skill, agency, discovery. "Matters" = the world remembers (game state). The creed is the north star; the **triple bind** below is how we enforce it feature-by-feature.

## The triple bind (master law)

Every **decision** and every **activity** must satisfy all three — or it's cut/redesigned:

1. **Learning** — it teaches something (saturation law above).
2. **Game state** — it *moves the world*: relationships, heat, Standing, routes, thread progress, or town state. **No cosmetic choices.** A decision the world doesn't remember is filler.
3. **Fun** — it's engaging: skill, agency, discovery, or delight.

**Test:** a "risky vs. safe" choice that only differs in flavor fails leg 2; a lore inspectable that changes nothing is fine as *optional* micro but a *required* beat must move state; an un-fun "lesson" fails leg 3 and must become a mechanic, a discovery, or a consequence. If a piece of content can't hit all three, it doesn't ship.

## The load-bearing gameplay systems (the pattern teachers)

Each core system is historically modeled so *playing it teaches the pattern* and success requires understanding it:

| Gameplay system | Historical pattern it makes load-bearing | SEs carried |
|---|---|---|
| **The Press** (you are a printer) | free press & propaganda in a republic; the ideas of the resistance | (21)(B), + vehicles the causes |
| **Economy / trade / errands** | mercantilism, free enterprise, non-importation/**boycott**, the port economy | (14)(A), (12)(A/C), (11)(A), (10)(C), (23)(E via Daughters of Liberty) |
| **The Watch / stealth / heat** (enforcement) | **oppression** — writs of assistance (searches), quartering, customs enforcement | (15)(C) grievances, felt not just read |

*(Cross-era patterns like regional economic differences (12)(C) only partly land in one city; the Archive completes them later — see `Curriculum-World-Map.md` spiral rules.)*

## Per-Act spread (keeps density ≈ today)

| Act | Gated facts (assessed) | Patterns active (load-bearing) |
|---|---|---|
| **A1 · 1765 Stamp Act** | Stamp Act · no representation · postwar revenue *(the 3 built today)* + Sam Adams intro | Press · economy/boycott/**mercantilism** · POV (Clarke) · **representation ★** · **port geography ★** |
| **A2 · 1770 Massacre** | the Boston Massacre · **Proclamation of 1763** · John Adams + Crispus Attucks | **grievances/oppression ★** (soldiers quartered, watch heightened, writs) · POV (soldiers' side) · citizenship (the trial/jury) · civic virtue |
| **A3 · 1773 Tea Party** | the Boston Tea Party · committees of correspondence · Old South town meeting | **representation ★** (town meeting) · **natural rights ★** · press · boycott/economy · women (Daughters of Liberty) |
| **A4 · 1774–75 Coercive Acts** | Intolerable Acts / **Port Act** · Lexington & Concord · Washington / Siege | **geography→economy ★** (harbor closed = punishment *felt*) · enforcement peak · grievances→rights synthesis |

★ = a dual-delivered pattern gets its one light Archive Sync in the Act where it's most active (representation in A1/A3, geography→economy in A1/A4, grievances in A2, natural rights in A3).

**Gated load per Act ≈ 3** (A1 keeps the proven 3; A2–A4 each carry ~3 gated facts + 4 dual-pattern Syncs spread across the whole chapter). Gameplay-only patterns cost **no** assessed payload — they ride on systems the player is already using. This is how Boston carries **~11 assessed facts + ~4 lightly-verified patterns + ~11 drilled-by-play patterns** without exceeding the ~25–30 min/Act budget.
