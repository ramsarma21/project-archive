# Boston Act 1 — Micro-Concept Ledger (Tier 2)

**Status: curated content list for the Tier-2 enrichment layer of Boston Act 1 (Stamp Act, 1765).**

This is the "curated dozen-ish per Act" locked in `Gameplay-Design.md` §3 — quality of coverage over breadth. These are **enrichment, never gates**: skipping every one of them still passes the Act. They are the concepts the reactive world (§8) can teach, and the pool the **CP1 debrief** samples from — **but only for the ones the student actually engaged** (fairness rule, §4).

**Reading this doc:**
- **Tier 1 (macro)** = the three required, path-invariant carriers, unchanged: **①** `RCC.DEBT_POLICY_INTRO` (war debt → revenue) · **②** `RCC.STAMP_INTERNAL_INTRO` (Stamp = internal tax on paper) · **③** `RCC.REPRESENTATION_CAUSE` (no taxation without representation) · plus the required *experience* `RCC.ORGANIZED_RESISTANCE_EVENT`. Micro concepts **enrich** one of these; they never replace or carry them.
- **Logs when** = the **tracked interaction** that credits the micro (focus-read, finished optional encounter, handled tagged object). Proximity / earshot / walking past **never** logs (FR-6, §3). Ambient chatter and posters only *seed* it in the air until a tracked interaction fires.
- **Delivered by** references the surfaces in `Day-1-Build-Script.md` (beats + the Appendix reactive layer) and the archetype rigs already in `animationManifest.ts` (`dockhand`, `agitator`, `taxclerk`, `towncrier`, `goodwife`).
- **TEKS tags are DRAFT** — proposed for the curriculum SME to confirm/approve before the bank is versioned (FR-8: content is authored/approved, never model-generated). Codes below use the Grade 8 U.S. History strand; verify exact codes/wording against the current adoption.

**Design invariants honored:** ≥1 *reliable* tracked surface per concept (so it can log at all), most with 2-3 so different play styles reach different ones; every concept ties to a macro it deepens; all deterministic; none block the spine.

## Enriches ① — war debt → revenue policy (why the taxes came)

### MICRO.SALUTARY_NEGLECT_END
- **One-line:** Before 1763 Britain loosely enforced colonial trade laws ("salutary neglect"); after the war it switched to *enforcing and taxing directly* — that change is what stung.
- **History:** The Seven Years'/French & Indian War (ended 1763) left Britain deeply in debt and holding new territory to defend. Parliament ended its hands-off habit and began raising revenue from the colonies (Sugar Act 1764, Stamp Act 1765).
- **Delivered by:** Custom House revenue proclamation (B7.5 focus-read, reinforces ①); Pike or Thomas ad-hoc line ("wasn't like this before the war").
- **Logs when:** focus-read the proclamation, **or** finish the Pike/Thomas ad-hoc exchange.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A) causes of the American Revolution.

### MICRO.PORT_TOWN_BOSTON
- **One-line:** Boston's economy ran on its harbor and shipping, so Crown trade duties and enforcement hit the town harder than most.
- **History:** Boston was one of the busiest colonial ports; merchants, dockhands, and shipping trades dominated. Revenue laws and customs enforcement landed squarely on that economy.
- **Delivered by:** dockhand haul side-job / dockhand-rigged encounter; harbor-edge knowledge-interactable; Thomas (merchant) B5.
- **Logs when:** finish the dockhand encounter/side-job, **or** handle the tagged harbor object, **or** the Thomas exchange.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.12 economic patterns / 8.4(A).

### MICRO.HARD_COIN_SCARCITY
- **One-line:** Hard coin (specie) was scarce in the colonies, and the stamp duties had to be paid in coin — squeezing people who mostly traded in paper and credit.
- **History:** Chronic specie shortage forced reliance on paper money and barter; requiring stamp payment in specie deepened resentment.
- **Delivered by:** Mercer's shop coin-vs-paper object (B4 knowledge-interactable); Pike aside.
- **Logs when:** handle the tagged shop object, **or** the Pike aside.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.12 / 8.13 economics.

## Enriches ② — Stamp Act = internal tax on paper (what it was)

### MICRO.PRINTERS_ROLE
- **One-line:** The tax fell directly on newspapers, pamphlets, and legal paper — so printers had a personal stake and became loud opponents.
- **History:** Every sheet of a newspaper and every legal/commercial document needed a stamp. Printers (like the Mercer shop) faced ruin, and colonial newspapers led the protest in print.
- **Delivered by:** Abigail + the press (B2 compound job); town notice board (B4.5); the print-shop trade objects (B4).
- **Logs when:** finish the B2 print job (already a tracked mechanic) **or** focus-read the notice board / handle the type case.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A) / 8.29 primary-source analysis.

### MICRO.VICE_ADMIRALTY_COURTS
- **One-line:** Accused Stamp Act violators could be tried in vice-admiralty courts — **no jury** — a direct hit on the right to a jury of peers.
- **History:** The Act let cases be heard in admiralty courts (judge, no jury), which colonists saw as stripping a basic English right.
- **Delivered by:** Pike (court clerk) B6/B6.5 line; Custom House interior.
- **Logs when:** finish the Pike exchange or the B6.5 sort nudge dialogue, **or** the Custom House tracked read.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A) causes; 8.19 rights/citizenship.

### MICRO.STAMP_WHAT_COUNTS
- **One-line:** The stamp was required on *printed and official* paper (newspapers, deeds, court writs, licenses, playing cards) — **not** on private letters or ordinary goods.
- **History:** The Act enumerated categories of taxed paper; the boundary (official/printed vs. private/handmade) is exactly the B6.5 sort's teaching point.
- **Delivered by:** B6.5 Pike sort (the demonstration itself doubles as the micro's richest surface); reinforced by the B4.5 notice.
- **Logs when:** the B6.5 sort completes (tracked), **or** focus-read the notice's schedule of duties.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A).
- **Note:** sits right beside macro ②; keep the question framing on the *boundary* (what needs it vs. not) so it enriches rather than restates the gated concept.

## Enriches ③ — no taxation without representation / organized resistance

### MICRO.ANDREW_OLIVER
- **One-line:** Andrew Oliver was the man appointed to distribute the stamps in Massachusetts; the Aug 14 crowd hung and burned his effigy to force him to quit — and the next day he resigned.
- **History:** Oliver (province secretary) was the designated stamp distributor. His effigy was hung on the elm, paraded, and burned; the crowd leveled a dockside building tied to him. He resigned publicly on Aug 15.
- **Delivered by:** the fixed event B11 (the effigy carries his placard — a focus-read on the effigy); agitator-rigged NPC encounter.
- **Logs when:** focus-read the effigy placard, **or** finish the agitator encounter.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(B) roles of significant individuals.

### MICRO.LIBERTY_TREE
- **One-line:** The great elm where the protest gathered became known as the "Liberty Tree" — a lasting rallying symbol of resistance.
- **History:** The elm at Hanover Square became the standing meeting point for Boston's resistance; "Liberty Tree" entered the movement's vocabulary.
- **Delivered by:** the elm as a knowledge-interactable at the B11 site; town-crier side-job line.
- **Logs when:** handle/read the tagged elm, **or** finish the crier stretch.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A); 8.15 symbols/citizenship.

### MICRO.LOYAL_NINE
- **One-line:** The protest wasn't a random riot — a small group of Boston tradesmen (the "Loyal Nine," forerunners of the Sons of Liberty) planned it.
- **History:** Nine artisans/shopkeepers organized the Aug 14 demonstration and coordinated the crowd; they were an early nucleus of what became the Sons of Liberty.
- **Delivered by:** agitator-rigged encounter; the tavern-note side-job (a boycott/organizing meeting).
- **Logs when:** finish the agitator encounter **or** deliver the tavern note (side-job completion).
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(B) significant individuals/groups.

### MICRO.EFFIGY_PROTEST
- **One-line:** Hanging, parading, and burning effigies was a common, deliberately theatrical form of 18th-century crowd protest — meant to shame an official publicly, usually stopping short of killing anyone.
- **History:** Boston's crowd traditions (e.g. Pope's Day) fed a ritual vocabulary of symbolic, targeted political theater; the Aug 14 action followed that script.
- **Delivered by:** observing the fixed event B11 (tracked observe); agitator line.
- **Logs when:** the B11 observe beat (tracked), **or** the agitator exchange.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A); 8.29 analysis (forms of protest).
- **Note:** supports the game's non-violent framing — protest as pressure/theater, not combat.

### MICRO.NON_IMPORTATION
- **One-line:** Colonists fought back economically too — merchants began agreeing to **stop importing British goods** to pressure Parliament through Britain's own merchants.
- **History:** Stamp Act resistance included early non-importation agreements among colonial merchants; the boycott tactic matured through 1767-70.
- **Delivered by:** Thomas (merchant) B5 haul conversation; the tavern-note side-job.
- **Logs when:** finish the Thomas exchange **or** complete the tavern-note side-job.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A); 8.12 economics.

### MICRO.NEWS_NETWORKS
- **One-line:** Ideas and news moved through informal networks — riders, printers, and taverns — the seedbed of what later became the committees of correspondence.
- **History:** Handbills, newspapers, post riders, and tavern talk knit the colonies' resistance together well before formal committees existed.
- **Delivered by:** the rider handoff (B10); the town-crier side-job; Abigail/press.
- **Logs when:** the B10 handoff (tracked) **or** the crier side-job completion.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A); 8.29 communication of information.

### MICRO.WRITS_OF_ASSISTANCE
- **One-line:** Customs officers could use "writs of assistance" — open-ended general search warrants — to search homes and shops for smuggled goods, which colonists saw as a violation of their rights.
- **History:** These general warrants (challenged by James Otis in 1761) had no specific target and didn't expire, fueling arguments about the rights of the accused and the reach of Crown enforcement.
- **Delivered by:** the B9 bag-inspection field tag (when challenged and you comply); Custom House interior.
- **Logs when:** the B9 comply/inspection beat (tracked) **or** a Custom House tracked read.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A); 8.19 rights/citizenship.
- **Note:** in-fiction the handbills aren't contraband yet — the point is the *power to search*, not this specific search.

### MICRO.LOYALIST_VIEW
- **One-line:** Not everyone agreed — Loyalists like Clarke held that Parliament had lawful authority and that mob action was dangerous and illegal.
- **History:** Colonial society was divided; many (officials, some merchants, Anglicans) believed in Parliamentary authority and feared crowd violence.
- **Delivered by:** Clarke B7 "hear him out" branch; a Loyalist unnamed-crowd line.
- **Logs when:** finish the Clarke "hear him out" exchange **or** the Loyalist crowd encounter.
- **TEKS (draft):** 8.4(A) multiple perspectives; 8.29 point of view.
- **Note:** the balance/critical-thinking concept — resistance was contested, not unanimous. Ties to the Political-read/Standing layer (§8).
